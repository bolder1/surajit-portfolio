# Asked in Order

Surajit's work told as the five questions a product designer asks on every project, in his order, each answered within a bar by a real artefact and read through one warm loupe of glass: his attention. The sixth question he asks himself, and the loupe turns on his own glasses. v3 walked his CV; v4 shows how he thinks, in the same direction, in his site's own colours, with type whose letterforms open under the lens.

## The story

A lamp clicks on in a dark room. A pen draws the frame from his three rules, and the first question slams in, cramped and shouting: what is this really for? The dot of its question mark lifts off and becomes a loupe. Outside the glass the world stays dark and compressed; under it, everything is lit, wide and calm. The loupe reads each question, then hunts for the answer in his real material: the research methods he uses, the console row the next operator inherits, the three destinations left from a canvas of everything, the five days of the Active Directory week, the shipped surfaces that fill the frame on the drop. Twenty-six real titles flash through the glass and the count lands on 58. Then the film stops dead. Colour drains, sound cuts, and the loupe flips onto the left lens of his glasses. The last question is his own: what do I leave off? His principle answers it, held longest, and the loupe shrinks to the full stop after "lasts".

## Through-line

One circular loupe of liquid glass, the only light in the room. Its 2 px ink rim is the stroke weight of the frame the pen draws at the open, so the film's two drawn objects obey his design-system rule. It has a job in every scene: dot, reader, finder, windows (the name), knife (Q3), viewport (the week; measured against the real cover on day 3), arrival (Q5), shutter (the burst), mirror (his glasses), full stop. The hero lens never scales (B's and C's rule): one radius, 420 px, and every grow or shrink is a clip-path reveal with the rim faded for at most 6 frames.

## Look: type, colour, light

Type: Bricolage Grotesque 800 for display; Newsreader Italic for his sentences only (56 px and up, one per scene, no hairlines); Martian Mono 500 for labels (22 to 24 px, typed with steps()). Signature move, the focus pull: outside the loupe, display type sits at optical size 12, width 75, 55% ink, cramped, its ink traps gaping (the notches cut into letter joins so small text does not clog); under the loupe it rides to optical size 96, width 100, open and smooth. The name does it once across the whole film, wide only on the end card. The serif italic breaks the ANTI-SLOP reflex rule on purpose: his voice must sound unlike the display face.

Colour, darkroom and cadmium: ground #0F0D0B · surface #1B1714 · ink #F0EBE1 · hero #E54D31 (vermilion: rim, lamp, arrow, ticks) · support #A79B88 · hit #FFD23F (cadmium, one beat on a landing) · key #FFB36B at 8 to 14% (a tungsten pool on the rim and his face, the one radial). Hero at most 2% of a frame, hit at most 1%, never on a hold. It is his site's near-black, bone and vermilion; his portrait is already lit in that red; his real screens are the only full colour, and the loupe's dispersion fringe the only spectral colour.

Glass: one Tier H loupe on v3's tested SVG recipe, full exposure inside, 35 to 55% outside; Tier B inward lenses for the name's glyph windows and the day tiles; glass never slides out and never holds nothing. Texture: 1.5% grain, the flashbulb on slams only, no scanlines, vignette, chroma or floor. Camera locked; the loupe moves; two camera moves in the film, the arrival and the flip. Kept from v3: the beat grid, the glass recipe, real screens and the real portrait, a sound per action, mono typed with steps(), the width move. Dropped: violet-black and the neon triad, beam, floor, graticule, CRT, chroma splits, Doto, the job-job-job spine, wireframes, the dither portrait.

References: Halt and Catch Fire (Elastic, 2014), a signal with a destination, so the loupe arrives on the drop where v3's beam only travelled; Bullitt (Pablo Ferro, 1968), footage inside letterforms, so the name holds his real screens; Neon Genesis Evangelion title cards (1995), the big card held, the small text flashed. Also Enter the Void (the burst), North by Northwest (the measured loupe), Dr. Strangelove (cropped type).

## Why this is better than v3

- The spine is how he thinks, five questions each answered by an artefact, instead of job, job, job. A hiring manager leaves with a point of view: he asked what not to build before he showed a screen.
- No drawn UI. Every screen is a capture; the name's letterforms hold his real work; sectors without captures are type.
- The type carries the thesis: cramped outside the lens, open inside it. v3 stretched a width axis as decoration.
- Key lines hold: questions 2.5 s or more, the principle 3.5 s, the end card 5 s. v3 held its principle for 2 s.
- The colour is his, and the dead stop and the turn onto his glasses are moments v3 did not have. Caveats: the questions are partly my words until he signs them, and dark plus one red accent is the trap ANTI-SLOP names; the fringe, the cadmium hit and the arrival hold it off.

## Risks and countermeasures

- Three questions are not verbatim his: each traces to a process step or his line, his verbatim sentence answers it, and question 0 asks him to rewrite.
- A growing hero lens breaks v3's no-scale rule: one radius, clip-path reveals with the rim faded for at most 6 f; one Tier H and three Tier B per frame.
- Light-theme screens full frame read as a screen recording: the arrival lasts 2 s between the lens opening and condensing; captures are cropped to a region, user chip out.
- Too much quiet: true silence is 15 f; the hum and sine carry the turn; 8 s without a beat in 56.
- Serif italic becomes the editorial template: only his sentences, 56 px or larger, no hairlines.
- Vermilion and ink traps shimmer in H.264: hero only on strokes of 3 px or more and type of 24 px or more, grain under everything, an encode test of F1 and F9 first.
- To confirm before render: "5 years" (the resume says 3+); "Identity & security" as the sector short form; day two as the home of "5 variants in 4 hours" (the source's day-two text says "multiple flow variants").

## Appendix: what the judges said

Both picked A. Hiring lens: A 46, B 44, C 39. Craft lens: A 48, B 47, C 39. Shared praise: questions answered by artefacts, a loupe with a job in every scene, the strategy beat (the canvas dims, three words stay), the turn onto his glasses.

Judge 1 wanted the burst limited to the 26 non-website titles so the reel does not shout brochure sites at 640 px; Judge 2 wanted all 58 minus Haller.Ai.
