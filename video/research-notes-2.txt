## Recipe: cinematic sub-boom impact (title hit)
The 'drop' that sells the name or title reveal. A sine with a fast pitch drop (120 to 36 Hz, tau 55 ms) gives a felt thump rather than a heard boom. Add a 4 ms high-passed noise click for laptop-speaker definition, a band-passed noise 'punch', a long low rumble, and tanh saturation so harmonics survive on small speakers. Verified: 2.8 s, 99% of energy below 80 Hz, RMS falls -10 to -32 dB across the four quarters of the sound.
BUILD: sr=48000; t=arange(2.8*sr)/sr; f=36+84*exp(-t/0.055); body=sin(2*pi*cumsum(f)/sr)*exp(-t/0.85)*minimum(1,t/0.002); click=butter_hp(noise,2500Hz,4th)*exp(-t/0.004)*0.6; punch=butter_bp(noise,70-450Hz,2nd)*exp(-t/0.09)*0.5; rumble=butter_lp(noise,180Hz,2nd)*exp(-t/1.1)*0.25; x=tanh(1.8*(sum))/tanh(1.8); then stereo convolution reverb (exponential-decay noise IR, RT60 2.2 s, damped at 3 kHz, wet 0.18); 300 ms fade-out; normalize to -1 dBFS. Layer kenney lowFrequency_explosion_000 at 0.5 and impactMetal_003 at 0.25 for the hero hit. Mix gain 0.9-1.0. It is the loudest event: one per act, maximum 3 in 45 s.

## Recipe: riser and reverse swell (anticipation into the drop)
The riser builds tension through the break (f900-959) and ends exactly on the hero hit. It has two layers. The noise layer is band-passed from 400 Hz to 9 kHz on an exponential curve. ## Sync and perception rules: hits on the frame, never early
Viewers notice sound that leads the picture much sooner than sound that lags it. ITU-R BT.1359 puts detectability at about 45 ms of audio lead versus about 125 ms of lag. So impacts go on the exact visual-contact frame or 1 frame after it, never before. Sounds with a slow attack follow different rules. Whooshes start early so their peak meets the motion peak. Risers and swells are placed by their END frame. Leave 1-2 frames of silence before a big hit (the riser's 4 ms cut does this). Never stack two impacts within 8 frames. Vary repeated UI sounds with a round-robin of 3-4 files plus toneFrequency jitter so 20+ clicks don't sound like a loop. Use frame-based easing curves (anticipation, overshoot) that match the audio envelope: hard-cut visuals for hard transients, eased moves for whooshes.
BUILD: Helpers: startForPeak(motionPeakFrame, sfxDurSec, peakRatio)=motionPeakFrame-Math.round(sfxDurSec*peakRatio*30); startForEnd(hitFrame, durSec)=hitFrame-Math.round(durSec*30). Round-robin: const CLICKS=['click_002','click_003','click_004','click_005']; src=staticFile(`sfx/${CLICKS[i%4]}.wav`); toneFrequency={0.92+random(`c${i}`)*0.16}. Verify sync by rendering a 5 s slice with `npx remotion render --frames=900-1050` and scrubbing.
